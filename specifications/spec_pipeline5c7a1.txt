1. **Problem Summary & Assumptions**
   - The goal is to develop an AI system that can classify MRI scans as either showing the presence or absence of a brain tumor. This is aimed at improving diagnostic accuracy and efficiency for radiologists and researchers. We assume that the dataset of 1,500 labeled MRI scans is well-organized and free of issues, and that the data is anonymized to comply with healthcare regulations.

2. **Data Specification**
   - The dataset consists of 1,500 labeled MRI scans stored in a research repository. Each scan is labeled to indicate whether a tumor is present or absent. The key variable is the tumor presence label. The data is assumed to be split into training, validation, and test sets, although specific paths are not provided.

     - `data_train`: "data/train"

     - `labels_location`: "data/labels"
   - The output directory will be set to "output/root".

4. **Preprocessing & Feature Engineering**
   - The MRI images will be preprocessed to ensure they are in a consistent format and size. This may include resizing the images to a standard dimension (e.g., 224x224 pixels) and normalizing pixel values. Data augmentation techniques (e.g., rotation, flipping) will be applied to increase the diversity of the training dataset and improve model robustness.

5. **Model Selection**
   - A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) will be selected for this binary classification task due to its effectiveness in image classification problems. A pre-trained model (e.g., ResNet50 or EfficientNet) will be used and fine-tuned on the MRI dataset to leverage transfer learning.

6. **Training Configuration**
   - The model will be trained using a binary cross-entropy loss function, suitable for binary classification tasks. The optimizer will be Adam, with a learning rate of 0.001. The training will run for a maximum of 50 epochs, with early stopping based on validation loss to prevent overfitting. A batch size of 32 will be used.
